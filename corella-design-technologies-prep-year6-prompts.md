# Corella Term Planning Prompts — Design Technologies (Prep–Year 6)

Complete, ready-to-use prompts for Corella. Every prompt is fully written out — nothing
to edit. Save each one in your Corella library or copy a box and send.

**Important:** These prompts now explicitly require a FULL unit plan with EVERY lesson
written out in detail, including lesson content, Ploughback, I Do, We Do and You Do.
If Corella stops early, reply: "Continue with Part 2 — write the remaining weeks and
lessons in full with Ploughback, I Do, We Do and You Do for every lesson."

## How to use

1. Find the band and term below.
2. Copy everything inside the code box.
3. Paste into Corella (or save as a named library prompt).
4. Send. No words need changing.

## Contents

Design Technologies — Prep–Year 1 (2 × 35 min per week)
- Term 3 — Designing for play: simple products from everyday materials
- Term 4 — Designing for others: gifts, packaging and useful classroom tools

Design Technologies — Years 2–4 (1 × 70 min per week)
- Term 3 — Materials, forces and structures: wind-powered or sheltered designs
- Term 4 — Designed solutions for a real need: organisers, feeders and recycled products

Design Technologies — Years 5–6 (1 × 70 min per week)
- Term 3 — Engineering systems and sustainable materials
- Term 4 — Design folio capstone: student-directed designed solution

## Semester 2 unit sequence

Prep–1: Designing for play → Designing for others.
Years 2–4: Materials, forces and structures → Designed solutions for a real need.
Years 5–6: Engineering systems and sustainable materials → Design folio capstone.

In every band, all year levels work on the same unit theme each term, with expectations
increasing by year level (spiral curriculum).

## Design Technologies (Prep–Year 1) — Term 3

Unit focus: Designing for play — simple products from everyday materials  
Timetable: 2 × 35-minute lessons per week

```text
Role

You are an expert Queensland Department of Education curriculum writer, experienced early-years and primary Design and Technologies specialist, Master Teacher, and specialist in teaching composite multi-age Prep–Year 1 classes in regional and remote Queensland schools.

Your task is to develop Term 3 only of a comprehensive Design Technologies teaching program for a composite Prep–Year 1 class, where both year levels are taught together in the same classroom.

This is Part 1 of a two-part Semester 2 program (Terms 3 and 4). Do not attempt to plan Term 4. Focus exclusively on creating the highest quality, most detailed, and practical Term 3 program possible. The completed program should be suitable for immediate implementation by a Queensland teacher in a regional/remote Queensland school with a composite Prep–Year 1 class.

⸻

Teaching Context

Design the program around the following teaching context:

Composite Prep–Year 1 Design Technologies class taught together in one classroom.
Students attend 2 lessons each week, each 35 minutes in duration (70 minutes total per week).
Assume a standard Queensland school term of approximately 10 teaching weeks.
Plan for approximately 20 short lessons per term.
Allow flexibility for interruptions such as public holidays, school events and student absences.
Every lesson must be tightly paced and achievable within 35 minutes, including: welcome, learning intention, activity, reflection, and pack-up.
Reserve approximately 3–5 minutes at the end of every lesson for reflection and pack-up.
Use play-based, hands-on making approaches appropriate for Prep and Year 1; keep tools simple and safe (scissors, glue, tape, recyclable materials, construction kits).
Sequence activities so the designed product can be completed within the available teaching time.
Prep learners follow Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 Design and Technologies Foundation Year; Year 1 learners follow Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 Design and Technologies Year 1.

⸻

Curriculum Requirements

Align the program with:

Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 Design and Technologies — Foundation Year and Year 1
Queensland Department of Education requirements
Safe use of classroom tools and materials for young children
Universal Design for Learning (UDL)
Explicit Teaching
High Impact Teaching Strategies
Positive Behaviour for Learning (PBL)
Differentiated instruction
Inclusive education practices
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures where authentic and appropriate
Sustainability where appropriate (including reuse of materials)

⸻

Teaching Philosophy

Design the entire term around a spiral curriculum and intentional play-based making.

All children should engage with the same design challenge simultaneously, with differentiation through product complexity, independence, vocabulary and recording expectations.

Differentiate through:
vocabulary, number of design steps, independence, materials choice, finish quality, and documentation (drawing/talking about designs).

⸻

Program Requirements

1. Term Overview

Produce a detailed overview including: Unit title, Unit rationale, Duration, Big Ideas, Essential Questions, Learning Intentions, Success Criteria, Achievement Standards, Australian Curriculum Version 9 Content Descriptors for Foundation Year and Year 1 (cited by code), General Capabilities, Cross-Curriculum Priorities, Required resources, Materials list, Tool and safety requirements, and Assessment schedule.
Explain the educational reasoning behind the unit design.

2. Skills Progression

Develop progression in: identifying a need or opportunity for play; exploring materials and their properties; generating design ideas through drawing and talking; selecting materials; making a simple product safely; testing and improving; evaluating whether the product works for its purpose.
Identify competencies for Prep and Year 1 during Term 3.

3. Major Project

Recommend one major designed product suitable for all Prep–Year 1 learners — Designing for play: a simple product from everyday and recycled materials (for example a puppet, toy vehicle, play prop, or construction toy).
Explain: why the project is suitable, what skills it develops, estimated timeline across approximately 20 short lessons, assessment opportunities, how it is differentiated for Prep and Year 1, extension opportunities, common misconceptions, and likely challenges.

4. Weekly Teaching Program

Develop a complete week-by-week teaching program for the entire term.
For every teaching week, provide:

Lesson 1 (35 minutes)
Include: Learning Intention; Success Criteria; Lesson timings (e.g. Welcome – 3 min, Learning intention – 2 min, Explicit teaching – 5 min, Making/design activity – 20 min, Reflection and pack-up – 5 min); Explicit instruction; Hands-on design/make activity; Formative assessment; Reflection.
Cite the specific Australian Curriculum Version 9 content descriptor codes addressed in this lesson.

Lesson 2 (35 minutes)
Include the same level of detail as Lesson 1, including cited curriculum codes.

5. Lesson Design

Each 35-minute lesson must be tightly scoped and achievable without rushing young children.

6. Differentiation

For every week's learning, explain practical adjustments for: Prep, Year 1, children requiring additional support, speech and language difficulties, EAL/D learners, and reasonable adjustments.

7. Assessment

Include: Diagnostic assessment; Formative observation; Summative designed product and simple design talk/drawing; Observation checklists; Work samples and photos; Portfolio suggestions; Evidence collection.

8. Classroom Routines

Develop routines for materials distribution, safe tool use, sharing, pack-up and reflection in short lessons.

9. Supporting Resources

Recommend: design idea cards, materials menus, safety posters for young makers, parent notes, intervention and extension activities, and teacher checklists.

⸻

You must write EVERY lesson in full.

Do NOT:
summarise weeks
```

```text
8. Classroom Routines and Management
```
