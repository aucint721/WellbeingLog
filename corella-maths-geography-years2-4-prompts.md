# Corella Term Planning Prompts — Mathematics & Geography (Years 2–4)

Complete, ready-to-use prompts for Corella. Every prompt is fully written out — nothing
to edit. Save each one in your Corella library or copy a box and send.

## How to use

1. Find the subject and term below.
2. Copy everything inside the code box.
3. Paste into Corella (or save as a named library prompt).
4. Send. No words need changing.

## Contents

Mathematics — Years 2–4 (5 × 70 min per week)
- Term 3 — Multiplicative thinking: multiplication, division and equal grouping
- Term 4 — Fractions, measurement and data

Geography — Years 2–4 (1 × 70 min per week)
- Term 3 — Places we live and connect with

## Semester 2 unit sequences

Mathematics: Multiplicative thinking → Fractions, measurement and data.
Geography: Places and connections → Environments and caring for places.

All Years 2–4 work on the same unit theme each term, with expectations increasing by
year level (spiral curriculum).

> Timetable assumptions:
> - Mathematics: 5 × 70-minute lessons per week (daily numeracy block)
> - Geography: 1 × 70-minute lesson per week
> If your timetable differs, tell Corella to adjust, or ask for rewritten prompts.

## Mathematics (Years 2–4) — Term 3

Unit focus: Multiplicative thinking — multiplication, division and equal grouping  
Timetable: 5 × 70-minute lessons per week

```text
Role

You are an expert Queensland Department of Education curriculum writer, experienced primary Mathematics specialist, Master Teacher, and specialist in teaching composite multi-age Years 2–4 classes in regional and remote Queensland schools.

Your task is to develop Term 3 only of a comprehensive Mathematics teaching program for a composite Years 2–4 class, where all three year levels are taught together in the same classroom.

This is Part 1 of a two-part Semester 2 program (Terms 3 and 4). Do not attempt to plan Term 4. Focus exclusively on creating the highest quality, most detailed, and practical Term 3 program possible. The completed program should be suitable for immediate implementation by a Queensland teacher in a regional/remote Queensland school with a composite Years 2–4 class.

⸻

Teaching Context

Design the program around the following teaching context:

Composite Years 2–4 Mathematics class taught together in one classroom.
Students attend 5 lessons each week, each 70 minutes in duration (350 minutes total instructional time per week).
Assume a standard Queensland school term of approximately 10 teaching weeks.
Plan for approximately 50 lessons per term.
Allow flexibility for interruptions such as public holidays, school events, assessment weeks and student absences.
Every lesson must include sufficient time for: welcome and settling, learning intention, explicit teaching, hands-on exploration with manipulatives, guided practice, independent or small-group work, reflection, and pack-up.
Reserve approximately 5–10 minutes at the end of every lesson for reflection and pack-up.
Lessons must be realistically achievable within 70 minutes without rushing students.
Use Explicit Teaching combined with hands-on manipulatives, number talks, problem-solving and fluency practice.
Include strategies to help absent students catch up without disrupting the flow of the class.
Sequence activities so major concepts and assessment tasks can be completed within the available teaching time, while allowing flexibility for students working at different rates.

⸻

Curriculum Requirements

Align the program with:

Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 Mathematics — Years 2, 3 and 4
Number, Algebra, Measurement, Space, Statistics and Probability as relevant to the unit
Queensland Department of Education requirements
Queensland numeracy teaching expectations
Universal Design for Learning (UDL)
Explicit Teaching
High Impact Teaching Strategies
Positive Behaviour for Learning (PBL)
Differentiated instruction
Inclusive education practices
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures where authentic and appropriate
Use of concrete materials and manipulatives as essential tools for learning

⸻

Teaching Philosophy

Design the entire term around a spiral curriculum.

All students should generally engage with the same multiplicative thinking themes and problem types simultaneously, with differentiation achieved through number range, representation sophistication, independence and reasoning depth rather than completely different programs.

Differentiate through:
number range, use of manipulatives, recording expectations, problem complexity, fluency expectations, independence, reasoning depth, and assessment expectations across Years 2, 3 and 4.

The aim is to build strong multiplicative thinking through equal grouping, arrays, sharing and relating multiplication and division.

⸻

Program Requirements

1. Term Overview

Produce a detailed overview including: Unit title, Unit rationale, Duration, Big Ideas, Essential Questions, Learning Intentions, Success Criteria, Achievement Standards, Australian Curriculum Version 9 Content Descriptors for Years 2, 3 and 4 (cited by code), General Capabilities, Cross-Curriculum Priorities, Required resources, Materials and manipulatives list, Classroom setup requirements, and Assessment schedule.
Explain the educational reasoning behind the unit design.

2. Skills and Concepts Progression

Develop a logical progression for Term 3 including:
equal groups and repeated addition
arrays and rows/columns
multiplication as equal grouping
division as sharing and grouping
relating multiplication and division
skip counting and multiplicative patterns
problem-solving with multiplicative situations
mathematical language and recording (including number sentences)
fluency with emerging multiplication facts appropriate to year level
Identify the specific competencies Years 2, 3 and 4 students should achieve during Term 3.

3. Major Unit Focus

Develop a detailed Term 3 unit focused on Multiplicative thinking: multiplication, division and equal grouping — suitable for all Years 2–4.
Explain:
why this unit is suitable for a composite class
what concepts and skills it develops
estimated timeline across approximately 10 weeks / 50 lessons
assessment opportunities
how learning is differentiated for Years 2, 3 and 4 (for example Year 2 focuses on equal groups, arrays and simple multiplication/division situations; Year 3 extends fact strategies and two-step problems; Year 4 deepens multiplicative reasoning, larger numbers and flexible strategies)
extension opportunities
common misconceptions
likely student challenges

4. Weekly Teaching Program

Develop a complete week-by-week teaching program for the entire term.
For every teaching week, divide the week into the five actual lessons taught.

Lesson 1 (70 minutes)
Lesson 2 (70 minutes)
Lesson 3 (70 minutes)
Lesson 4 (70 minutes)
Lesson 5 (70 minutes)

For each lesson include:
Learning Intention
Success Criteria
Lesson timings (e.g. Welcome – 5 min, Number talk/warm-up – 10 min, Explicit teaching – 15 min, Guided practice – 15 min, Independent/small-group work – 15 min, Reflection – 10 min)
Starter or number talk
Review of prior learning
Explicit instruction
Teacher modelling
Guided practice
Independent or small-group activities
Use of manipulatives
Literacy and oral language focus (mathematical vocabulary)
Numeracy focus
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives (where authentic)
Required resources
Teacher preparation
Formative assessment
Reflection
Exit ticket or sharing activity

Cite the specific Australian Curriculum Version 9 content descriptor codes addressed in each lesson.

Across each week, balance fluency, conceptual understanding and problem-solving rather than five identical lesson types.

5. Lesson Design

Each lesson should follow an Explicit Teaching model combined with hands-on exploration, with realistic timings achievable within 70 minutes.
Include regular opportunities for number talks, guided groups and independent practice.

6. Differentiation

For every week's learning, explain practical, classroom-ready adjustments for: Year 2, Year 3, Year 4, high-achieving students, students requiring additional learning support, students with literacy difficulties, EAL/D learners, and students requiring reasonable adjustments. Provide concrete strategies rather than general statements.
Include practical advice for managing guided maths groups in a composite class.

7. Assessment

Include:
Diagnostic assessment
Formative assessment through observation, conversation and work samples
Summative assessment opportunities
Teacher observation checklists
Student self-assessment where age-appropriate
Evidence collection suggestions
Rubric or checklist recommendations differentiated across Years 2–4
Moderation notes

8. Classroom Routines and Management

Develop consistent routines for: entering the classroom, attendance, maths rotations, manipulatives distribution and collection, guided group transitions, pack-up, and end-of-lesson reflection suitable for a composite Years 2–4 class.

9. Supporting Resources

Recommend: printable worksheets, array cards, equal-group materials, game templates, fluency grids, intervention activities, extension activities, homework (where appropriate), parent communication notes, and teacher checklists.

⸻

Output Requirements

Present the response using professional headings, tables and clearly organised sections suitable for immediate classroom use.
Where possible, explain the educational reasoning behind recommendations rather than simply listing information.
Provide sufficient detail so that another Queensland Mathematics teacher could confidently teach the entire term using this document.
Do not abbreviate sections or provide summaries.
Produce a comprehensive planning document suitable for direct implementation in a regional/remote Queensland school.

Focus exclusively on Term 3. Do not begin planning Term 4.

At the conclusion of the document, provide recommendations for Term 4 based on the intended progression of student knowledge, skills and competencies — but do not develop the Term 4 program itself.
```

Timetable: 5 × 70-minute lessons per week

```text
You are an expert Queensland Department of Education curriculum writer, experienced primary Mathematics specialist, Master Teacher, and specialist in teaching composite multi-age Years 2–4 classes in regional and remote Queensland schools.

Your task is to develop Term 4 only of a comprehensive Mathematics teaching program for a composite Years 2–4 class, where all three year levels are taught together in the same classroom.

This is Part 2 of a two-part Semester 2 program. Assume students have completed Term 3 (Multiplicative thinking: multiplication, division and equal grouping) and can use equal groups, arrays and simple multiplication/division situations with growing confidence. what concepts and skills it develops

assessment opportunities
how learning is differentiated for Years 2, 3 and 4 (for example Year 2 focuses on halves/quarters, informal measurement and simple picture graphs; Year 3 extends fraction families, metric units and data displays; Year 4 deepens equivalent fractions where appropriate, area/perimeter beginnings, and more sophisticated data interpretation)
extension opportunities
common misconceptions
likely student challenges
```

## Geography (Years 2–4) — Term 3

Unit focus: Places we live and connect with  
Timetable: 1 × 70-minute lesson per week

```text
Role

You are an expert Queensland Department of Education curriculum writer, experienced primary Humanities and Social Sciences (Geography) specialist, Master Teacher, and specialist in teaching composite multi-age Years 2–4 classes in regional and remote Queensland schools.

Your task is to develop Term 3 only of a comprehensive Geography teaching program for a composite Years 2–4 class, where all three year levels are taught together in the same classroom.

This is Part 1 of a two-part Semester 2 Geography program (Terms 3 and 4). Do not attempt to plan Term 4. Focus exclusively on creating the highest quality, most detailed, and practical Term 3 program possible. The completed program should be suitable for immediate implementation by a Queensland teacher in a regional/remote Queensland school with a composite Years 2–4 class.

⸻

Teaching Context

Design the program around the following teaching context:

Composite Years 2–4 Geography class taught together in one classroom.
Students attend 1 lesson each week, each 70 minutes in duration.
Assume a standard Queensland school term of approximately 10 teaching weeks.
Plan for approximately 10 lessons per term.
Allow flexibility for interruptions such as public holidays, school events, assessment weeks and student absences.
Every lesson must include sufficient time for: welcome, learning intention, explicit teaching, inquiry or map/source activity, guided practice, independent or collaborative work, reflection, and pack-up.
Lessons must be realistically achievable within 70 minutes.
Use inquiry-based learning, Explicit Teaching of geographical concepts and skills, and literacy-rich activities.
Include local and Australian place contexts suitable for regional/remote Queensland students.
Homework may be used sparingly to extend inquiry where appropriate given only one lesson per week.

⸻

Curriculum Requirements

Align the program with:

Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 Humanities and Social Sciences: Geography — Years 2, 3 and 4
Queensland Department of Education requirements
Universal Design for Learning (UDL)
Explicit Teaching
High Impact Teaching Strategies
Positive Behaviour for Learning (PBL)
Differentiated instruction
Inclusive education practices
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures where authentic and appropriate
Ethical use of maps, images and information

⸻

Teaching Philosophy

Design the entire term around a spiral curriculum and geographical inquiry.

All students should investigate the same overarching inquiry questions about places and connections, with differentiation through source/map complexity, depth of explanation, written expectations and independence rather than completely different topics.

Differentiate through:
map and source complexity, depth of geographical reasoning, written response length and sophistication, independence, use of evidence, and assessment expectations across Years 2, 3 and 4.

⸻

Program Requirements

1. Term Overview

Develop a logical progression including:
locating places using maps, globes and simple atlases/digital maps
using geographical language (place, space, environment, connection, location, direction, distance)
describing features of places
comparing places
identifying how people are connected to places
representing information in simple maps, tables and labelled diagrams
drawing conclusions from geographical sources
Identify the specific competencies Years 2, 3 and 4 students should achieve during Term 3.

3. Major Unit Focus

Develop a detailed Term 3 unit focused on Places we live and connect with — suitable for all Years 2–4, aligned with Australian Curriculum Version 9 Geography.
Explain:
why this unit is suitable for a composite class in regional/remote Queensland
what knowledge and skills it develops
how local, Australian and other places can be included authentically
estimated timeline across approximately 10 lessons
assessment opportunities
how learning is differentiated for Years 2, 3 and 4 (for example Year 2 focuses on personal/local connections to places; Year 3 compares similarities and differences between places; Year 4 extends to why places are important and how people are connected across places)
extension opportunities
common misconceptions
likely student challenges

4. Weekly Teaching Program

Develop a complete week-by-week teaching program for the entire term (10 weeks, 1 lesson per week).

Lesson (70 minutes)
Include: Learning Intention; Success Criteria; Lesson timings (e.g. Welcome and review – 5 min, Explicit teaching – 15 min, Inquiry/map activity – 25 min, Guided practice – 15 min, Reflection and exit – 10 min); Starter activity; Review of prior learning; Explicit instruction; Teacher modelling of geographical skills; Guided source/map analysis; Independent or collaborative inquiry tasks; Literacy focus; Numeracy connections (direction, distance, scale beginnings); Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander perspectives (where authentic); Required resources; Teacher preparation; Formative assessment; Reflection; Exit ticket or summary task.
Cite the specific Australian Curriculum Version 9 content descriptor codes addressed in this lesson.

5. Lesson Design

Each lesson should follow an Explicit Teaching and inquiry model with realistic timings achievable within 70 minutes.

6. Differentiation

For every week's learning, explain practical, classroom-ready adjustments for: Year 2, Year 3, Year 4, high-achieving students, students requiring additional learning support, students with literacy difficulties, EAL/D learners, and students requiring reasonable adjustments. Provide concrete strategies rather than general statements.

7. Assessment

Include:
Diagnostic assessment
Formative assessment
Summative assessment opportunities (for example a place study, labelled map task, or inquiry response)
Teacher observation
Rubric recommendations differentiated across Years 2–4
Self and peer assessment where appropriate
Evidence collection
Moderation notes

8. Classroom Routines and Management

Develop consistent routines for: entering the classroom, distribution of maps and devices, collaborative group work, discussion protocols, pack-up, and end-of-lesson reflection.

9. Supporting Resources

Recommend: map packs, place photo sets, graphic organisers, vocabulary lists, writing scaffolds, local place investigation templates, extension readings, intervention scaffolds, homework tasks (where appropriate), and teacher checklists.
```

Timetable: 1 × 70-minute lesson per week

```text
assessment opportunities

extension opportunities
common misconceptions
likely student challenges
```
