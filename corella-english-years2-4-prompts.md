## English (Years 2–4) — Term 3

Unit focus: Stories that matter — narrative literature and creating narratives  
Timetable: 5 × 70-minute lessons per week

```text
Role

You are an expert Queensland Department of Education curriculum writer, experienced primary English specialist, Master Teacher, and specialist in teaching composite multi-age Years 2–4 classes in regional and remote Queensland schools.

Your task is to develop Term 3 only of a comprehensive English teaching program for a composite Years 2–4 class, where all three year levels are taught together in the same classroom.

This is Part 1 of a two-part Semester 2 program (Terms 3 and 4). Do not attempt to plan Term 4. Focus exclusively on creating the highest quality, most detailed, and practical Term 3 program possible. The completed program should be suitable for immediate implementation by a Queensland teacher in a regional/remote Queensland school with a composite Years 2–4 class.

⸻

Teaching Context

Design the program around the following teaching context:

Composite Years 2–4 English class taught together in one classroom.
Students attend 5 lessons each week, each 70 minutes in duration (350 minutes total instructional time per week).
Assume a standard Queensland school term of approximately 10 teaching weeks.
Plan for approximately 50 lessons per term.
Allow flexibility for interruptions such as public holidays, school events, assessment weeks and student absences.
Every lesson must include sufficient time for: welcome and settling, learning intention, explicit teaching, guided practice, independent or small-group work, sharing/reflection, and pack-up.
Reserve approximately 5–10 minutes at the end of every lesson for reflection and pack-up.
Lessons must be realistically achievable within 70 minutes without rushing students.
Use Explicit Teaching combined with rich literature experiences, guided reading/writing, shared reading, independent reading, and oral language opportunities.
Balance reading, writing, speaking and listening across the week.
Include strategies to help absent students catch up without disrupting the flow of the class.
Sequence the unit so students can complete major reading and writing assessment tasks within the available teaching time, while allowing flexibility for students working at different rates.

⸻

Curriculum Requirements

Align the program with:

Australian Curriculum Version 9.0 English — Years 2, 3 and 4
Language, Literature and Literacy strands
Queensland Department of Education requirements
Queensland literacy teaching expectations
Universal Design for Learning (UDL)
Explicit Teaching
High Impact Teaching Strategies
Positive Behaviour for Learning (PBL)
Differentiated instruction
Inclusive education practices
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Histories and Cultures where authentic and appropriate
Ethical use of texts and information

⸻

Teaching Philosophy

Design the entire term around a spiral curriculum.

All students should generally engage with the same core texts, themes and writing purposes simultaneously, with differentiation achieved through text complexity, scaffolding, independence, vocabulary, sentence complexity and response expectations rather than completely different programs.

Differentiate through:
text complexity, reading support, writing length and sophistication, oral language expectations, independence, vocabulary, and assessment expectations across Years 2, 3 and 4.

The aim is to build confident readers and writers of narrative through high-quality literature and explicit teaching of craft.

⸻

Program Requirements

1. Term Overview

Produce a detailed overview including: Unit title, Unit rationale, Duration, Big Ideas, Essential Questions, Learning Intentions, Success Criteria, Achievement Standards, Australian Curriculum Version 9 Content Descriptors for Years 2, 3 and 4 (cited by code) across Language, Literature and Literacy, General Capabilities, Cross-Curriculum Priorities, Required resources, Core text recommendations (picture books, short novels, poems and multimodal texts suitable for a composite Years 2–4 class), Classroom setup requirements, and Assessment schedule.
Explain the educational reasoning behind the unit design.

2. Skills Progression

Develop a logical progression for Term 3 including:
reading and discussing narrative literature
understanding characters, setting, plot and theme
vocabulary and language features of narrative (including noun groups, verb groups, dialogue, descriptive language)
phonics, spelling and word study linked to texts where appropriate
sentence-level grammar for narrative writing
planning, drafting, revising and publishing a narrative
oral storytelling and reading aloud
listening and responding to others’ ideas
Identify the specific competencies Years 2, 3 and 4 students should achieve during Term 3.

3. Major Unit Focus

Develop a detailed Term 3 unit focused on Stories that matter: narrative literature and creating narratives — suitable for all Years 2–4.
Explain:
why this unit is suitable for a composite class
what knowledge and skills it develops
recommended core texts and how they can be used across year levels
estimated timeline across approximately 10 weeks / 50 lessons
assessment opportunities
how learning is differentiated for Years 2, 3 and 4 (for example Year 2 creates a shorter illustrated narrative with strong oral rehearsal; Year 3 develops clearer plot structure and paragraphing; Year 4 crafts a more sophisticated narrative with stronger characterisation, dialogue and revision)
extension opportunities
common misconceptions
likely student challenges

4. Weekly Teaching Program — FULL UNIT PLAN AND EVERY LESSON IN FULL DETAIL

You must produce a COMPLETE unit plan for the entire term.
You must write EVERY lesson in full.

Do NOT:
summarise weeks
provide only sample weeks
say "continue as above" or "similar to previous lesson"
collapse multiple lessons into one outline
stop after Week 1 or Week 2 with a summary of the rest

For every teaching week, divide the week into the five actual lessons taught, and write each lesson separately:

Lesson 1 (70 minutes)
Lesson 2 (70 minutes)
Lesson 3 (70 minutes)
Lesson 4 (70 minutes)
Lesson 5 (70 minutes)

CRITICAL — for EVERY lesson include ALL of the following in full detail:

A. Week number, lesson number and duration
B. Learning Intention
C. Success Criteria
D. Australian Curriculum Version 9 content descriptor codes (cited by code)
E. Lesson content / focus — the specific content being taught today (not a vague theme)
F. Exact lesson timings for each phase (must add up to 70 minutes)
G. Ploughback — explicit ploughback of prior learning: what previous knowledge/skills are being revisited, how the ploughback is taught, and for how many minutes
H. I Do (Teacher modelling) — exact teacher instructions: what the teacher says and does, what is modelled, and the examples used
I. We Do (Guided practice) — exact guided practice instructions: what teacher and students do together, prompts used, and checks for understanding
J. You Do (Independent / small-group practice) — exact tasks students complete, including differentiation notes for Year 2, Year 3 and Year 4
K. Resources and materials needed for that lesson
L. Formative assessment / checks for understanding during the lesson
M. Reflection / exit ticket
N. Plough-forward note — what will be revisited or built on in the next lesson

Across each week, ensure a balanced mix of reading, writing, speaking and listening rather than five identical lesson types.
Write Weeks 1–10 completely. Every lesson must have unique content detail.

5. Lesson Design

Every lesson must follow Explicit Teaching using the I Do / We Do / You Do model.
Every lesson must include ploughback of prior learning.
Include regular opportunities for modelled, shared, guided and independent reading and writing.
Timings must be realistic and achievable within 70 minutes.

6. Differentiation

For every week's learning, explain practical, classroom-ready adjustments for: Year 2, Year 3, Year 4, high-achieving students, students requiring additional learning support, students with literacy difficulties, EAL/D learners, and students requiring reasonable adjustments. Provide concrete strategies rather than general statements.
Include practical advice for managing guided reading/writing groups in a composite class.

7. Assessment

Include:
Diagnostic assessment (reading and writing)
Formative assessment
Summative assessment opportunities (for example a published narrative and reading response or literature discussion task)
Teacher observation checklists
Running records or reading conference notes where appropriate
Work sample collection
Student self-assessment and peer feedback where age-appropriate
Evidence collection suggestions
Rubric recommendations differentiated across Years 2–4
Moderation notes

8. Classroom Routines and Management

Develop consistent routines for: entering the classroom, attendance, literacy rotations, guided group transitions, independent reading, writing workshops, sharing circles, pack-up, and end-of-lesson reflection suitable for a composite Years 2–4 class.

9. Supporting Resources

Recommend: core text lists, vocabulary lists, graphic organisers for narrative planning, sentence starters and scaffolds, spelling/word-study activities, intervention activities, extension activities, homework (where appropriate), parent communication notes, and teacher checklists.

⸻

Output Requirements

You must produce a FULL unit plan AND a FULL lesson-by-lesson teaching sequence for every lesson in the term.
Every lesson must include unique lesson content plus I Do, We Do, You Do and Ploughback sections written out in full.

Do NOT abbreviate, summarise, sample, or truncate.
Do NOT write "similar to previous lesson", "continue this pattern", or "repeat structure from Week 1".
Do NOT provide only exemplar weeks.

If a response length limit is reached, continue in a clearly labelled Part 2, Part 3, and further parts until every lesson for Weeks 1–10 is complete. Do not stop early with a summary.

Present the response using professional headings, tables and clearly organised sections suitable for immediate classroom use.
Where possible, explain the educational reasoning behind recommendations rather than simply listing information.
Another Queensland English teacher must be able to teach every lesson from this document without inventing missing steps.
Produce a comprehensive planning document suitable for direct implementation in a regional/remote Queensland school.

Focus exclusively on Term 3. Do not begin planning Term 4.

At the conclusion of the document, provide recommendations for Term 4 based on the intended progression of student knowledge, skills and competencies — but do not develop the Term 4 program itself.
```

Unit focus: Voices and viewpoints — persuasive and multimodal texts  
Timetable: 5 × 70-minute lessons per week
